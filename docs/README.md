# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This directory centralizes OctoAcme’s project management processes and serves as the starting point for understanding how work is initiated, planned, executed, shipped, and improved over time. The project model is designed to keep teams aligned around customer value, clear ownership, and measurable outcomes while remaining flexible enough for iterative delivery.

## Overview of OctoAcme’s project management processes

OctoAcme follows a structured lifecycle that begins with initiation and validation. New work is assessed through a lightweight one-pager that captures the business problem, goals, success metrics, stakeholders, timeline, risks, and resource needs. Once the initiative is approved, teams move into planning, creating prioritized backlog items with acceptance criteria, estimates, owners, and dependencies so the work can be broken into manageable, shippable increments. The planning stage also defines the definition of done, milestones, and release expectations.

During execution, the team delivers in small increments while using a regular cadence of standups, reviews, and weekly project syncs to track progress and resolve blockers. OctoAcme’s roles are intentionally clear: Product Managers define the problem and priorities, Project Managers coordinate plans and communications, developers build and validate the solution, QA validates the quality bar, and stakeholders provide input and approvals. The organization also emphasizes data-informed decisions, continuous iteration, and psychological safety so teams can adapt and learn without losing accountability.

Risk management and communication are treated as core delivery practices rather than administrative tasks. Teams maintain a risk register, monitor dependencies, and escalate issues through a defined path from the delivery team to the PM, Product Lead, and sponsor as needed. Regular stakeholder communication, status updates, and clear escalation paths help maintain visibility and reduce preventable disruption. Quality assurance is embedded in the process through unit, integration, and smoke testing, CI validation, security scans, and manual QA where appropriate. Pull requests are expected to be small and reviewable, with issue links, acceptance criteria, and required approvals before merge.

Once work is ready to ship, OctoAcme uses a standard release and deployment process that includes staging validation, smoke tests, rollback planning, and post-deploy verification to reduce production risk. After each sprint, milestone, or incident, the team conducts a retrospective to capture what went well, what could improve, and which actions should be tracked as follow-up work. The result is a repeatable operating model that balances speed, quality, stakeholder alignment, and continuous improvement.
