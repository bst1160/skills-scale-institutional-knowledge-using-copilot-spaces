# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This repository contains the core project management process documents used by OctoAcme to plan, execute, and improve work across teams.

## Project management overview

OctoAcme’s project management approach follows a clear lifecycle: initiation, planning, execution, release, and closeout. During initiation, the team validates the business problem, sets success metrics, identifies stakeholders, and creates a lightweight one-pager with the goal, timeline, risks, and resource needs. Once approved, planning turns the concept into a backlog with milestones, acceptance criteria, dependencies, and a definition of done. Execution then focuses on delivering work in small, testable increments using a project board, pull requests, and regular team reviews. At the end of each sprint or milestone, the team captures lessons learned and turns them into improvements for future work.

The process relies on clear roles and responsibilities. Product Managers define the problem and prioritize work, Project Managers coordinate scheduling, risks, communication, and dependencies, and developers build and validate the solution. QA and testing support quality validation, stakeholders provide input and approvals, and the team operates around shared artifacts such as the project charter, roadmap, backlog, risk register, and retrospective notes. This emphasis on clear ownership, measurable outcomes, and cross-functional collaboration keeps work aligned and decision-making transparent.

Communication is regular and structured. The team uses weekly PM and PdM syncs, delivery-team standups, milestone or stakeholder updates, and escalation paths when issues require attention. Risks and blockers are tracked in a risk register and escalated from team-level triage to PM, Product Lead, and sponsor-level involvement when the impact is significant. For incidents or high-risk events, communication includes a summary, actions underway, expected timeline, and a blameless retrospective. This creates a consistent source of truth for status and helps teams respond quickly to unexpected issues.

Quality assurance is built into the workflow rather than left to the end. The team emphasizes unit and integration testing, end-to-end smoke tests for critical flows, CI checks for tests and security scans, PR review requirements, and manual QA when needed. Release work follows a defined checklist for staging, deployment verification, and rollback preparedness, while retrospective and continuous improvement practices ensure that action items are tracked and reviewed over time. Together, these practices reduce risk, improve predictability, and support repeatable delivery.
